# FRAME: 50 carousel concepts

Read [BRIEF.md](BRIEF.md) first for voice, palette, and the claims that cannot
be made. This file is the backlog.

Each entry gives a **hook** (slide 1, six words or fewer), a **slide count**,
and the **arc**. Feed the master prompt below plus one entry to Nano Banana Pro.

## The master prompt

Paste this, then append the entry you want. Generate slide 1 first, then feed
that image back as a style reference for every following slide, changing only
the copy. Independently generated slides will not match.

```
You are generating one slide of an Instagram carousel for FRAME, a real-world
hide and seek game where players must photograph their hiding spot every few
minutes to prove they are still hidden.

CANVAS
1080 x 1350, 4:5 portrait. All text inside the middle 85 percent so nothing is
cropped by the platform.

COLOUR
Background: near-black #0A0A0C, edge to edge, flat.
Body text: off-white #F4F4F2.
Accent: acid yellow-green #C8FF2E, on exactly ONE element per slide and no
more. It marks the single most important thing on the slide.
Alert red #FF4438 and amber #FFB020 only for genuine warnings.
Never put white text on the acid green. Contrast is 1.18:1 and unreadable. On
green, use near-black #0A0A0C.

TYPE
Headlines in a heavy geometric sans-serif, tight tracking, sentence case or
caps. Small labels in a monospace, ALL CAPS, wide letter spacing of roughly 2
pixels at 11 point. That letterspaced mono is the signature of the brand and
should appear on every slide as a small label, eyebrow, or slide counter.
Numbers, timers, and counts in a monospace with tabular figures.

LAYOUT
Generous negative space. Content sits left-aligned on a clear grid. One idea
per slide. Optional thin 1px rules in #232329 to divide sections. Optional
small square corner brackets in acid green framing a key element, which is the
brand's motif.

STYLE
Flat, graphic, industrial. Reads like a piece of equipment or a control panel,
not a friendly consumer app. No gradients, no glow, no drop shadows, no 3D, no
stock photography, no illustrated characters, no emoji.

RULES
No em-dashes anywhere in the copy. Use periods, commas, colons, or a middle dot.
No exclamation marks.
Do not claim the app recognises faces or identifies people.
Do not imply you can play with strangers.
Do not depict hiding on private property, near traffic, or on train tracks.

SLIDE CONTENT
[paste the slide's headline and body here]
```

## A. The mechanic (start here, these are the ads)

**1. Sixty seconds** · 6 slides
Hook: `Your phone just went off.`
Arc: the tick fires → you have 60 seconds → back camera → front camera → it
goes to the seeker → miss it and you are out. This is the single best
explanation of the product and should be the pinned post.

**2. Blacked out** · 5 slides
Hook: `The worst way to lose.`
Arc: being tagged is fine, someone found you → being BLACKED OUT means you
failed to prove you were hiding → the whole party gets told → it is on your
stats → the game is designed to make this embarrassing.

**3. What the app checks** · 7 slides
Hook: `It knows you covered the lens.`
Arc: one slide per signal, blur, brightness, detail, uniformity, reuse,
timestamp. Final slide: none of these look at people, only at the image.

**4. Two cameras, one flow** · 4 slides
Hook: `Back camera. Then front.`
Arc: why both, what each proves, why gallery upload does not exist, why the
capture must be live.

**5. The reveal tick** · 5 slides
Hook: `Every ten minutes you light up.`
Arc: seeker gets all positions → for 30 seconds → then they fade → you get
told it happened but not what they saw → react or do not.

**6. Why the zone shrinks** · 4 slides
Hook: `The map is getting smaller.`
Arc: last third of the round → 75, 50, 35 percent → announced 60 seconds
early → nowhere left to sit still.

**7. You cannot be tagged from across the street** · 5 slides
Hook: `GPS is not good enough.`
Arc: GPS is off by 5 to 20 metres in cities → a false tag ends a round
unfairly → so a tag needs a Bluetooth handshake or a code typed face to face.

**8. Anatomy of a round** · 8 slides
Hook: `45 minutes. Here is all of it.`
Arc: lobby → seeker assigned → cooldown → disperse → first check-in → first
reveal → zone shrink → survive or get cleared.

**9. The 60 second window** · 3 slides
Hook: `Not five minutes. Sixty seconds.`
Arc: short, punchy, the timer is the whole tension.

**10. One retry** · 4 slides
Hook: `Fail once. You get 30 seconds.`
Arc: failed check → plain-language reason → one retry with extra time → fail
again and you are gone.

## B. Hiding strategy

**11. Spots that pass** · 7 slides
Hook: `Five spots that survive a check.`
Arc: one per slide, alley, stairwell, garage, hedge, bus shelter, plus why
each has enough visual detail to pass.

**12. Spots that will end you** · 7 slides
Hook: `These will get you blacked out.`
Arc: pressed against a blank wall, inside a bag, a pitch-dark room, under a
coat. Each fails a specific check and the slide names which.

**13. Do not find one hole and sit in it** · 5 slides
Hook: `Sitting still is how you lose.`
Arc: the old GPS-game strategy → why photos break it → the same background
twice is a tell → keep rotating.

**14. Hiding in plain sight** · 5 slides
Hook: `Crowds beat corners.`
Arc: a busy plaza gives you texture, exits, and cover; a dead-end alley gives
you one way out.

**15. The parking garage problem** · 4 slides
Hook: `Garages are a trap.`
Arc: dim, evenly lit, low detail, and no signal for the tick notification.

**16. Verticality** · 4 slides
Hook: `Seekers do not look up.`
Arc: stairwells, mezzanines, upper decks, and the tradeoff that you can be
cornered.

**17. Exit count** · 4 slides
Hook: `Count your exits first.`
Arc: pick spots with two ways out, before you think about whether it hides you.

**18. The first two minutes** · 5 slides
Hook: `Where you go first decides it.`
Arc: seeker cooldown is your only free movement → spend it moving far, not
hiding well.

**19. Rain, dusk, night** · 5 slides
Hook: `Dark is not automatically safer.`
Arc: night helps you hide from a person and hurts you with the camera check.
The tradeoff is the interesting part.

**20. Do not run** · 3 slides
Hook: `Run and the app stops you.`
Arc: above 10 mph the round suspends. This is a safety feature, and it is also
strategy.

## C. The photo skill (the most ownable angle)

**21. Photos that prove nothing** · 6 slides
Hook: `Pass the check. Give away nothing.`
Arc: the core skill. Fill the frame with texture, avoid landmarks, avoid
signage, avoid skyline, avoid anything with a name on it.

**22. What gives you away** · 6 slides
Hook: `Six things that name your location.`
Arc: street signs, shop fronts, distinctive paving, a skyline, a bus number,
a reflection.

**23. Reflections** · 4 slides
Hook: `Check the window behind you.`
Arc: the most common accidental giveaway.

**24. Framing tight** · 4 slides
Hook: `Get closer than feels right.`
Arc: a tight frame on a textured surface passes every check and tells the
seeker almost nothing.

**25. The front camera problem** · 5 slides
Hook: `The front camera is the hard one.`
Arc: you cannot hide behind a wall of texture, you have to show some of where
you are. How to choose what.

**26. Seekers read backgrounds** · 5 slides
Hook: `Your seeker is a detective now.`
Arc: from the seeker's side, what actually gives a hider away in a photo.

**27. Same spot, different photo** · 4 slides
Hook: `You can stay. Just move the camera.`
Arc: reuse detection is about the image, not the location.

## D. Failure and humour (highest shareability)

**28. Blackout compilation** · 6 slides
Hook: `Six people who did not make it.`
Arc: short caption per failure, escalating absurdity.

**29. Excuses that do not work** · 6 slides
Hook: `The app does not care.`
Arc: my phone died, I had no signal, I was in a lift, I fell asleep. Each with
the actual consequence.

**30. Battery** · 4 slides
Hook: `Start above 40 percent.`
Arc: GPS plus camera plus push for 45 minutes. A dead phone is a loss.

**31. Things people photograph by accident** · 5 slides
Hook: `Do not send this to the seeker.`
Arc: your own front door, a street sign, your friend, the inside of a pocket.

**32. The pocket check-in** · 3 slides
Hook: `That is a pocket. You are out.`
Arc: single joke, tightly told.

**33. Group chat aftermath** · 5 slides
Hook: `The argument starts after the round.`
Arc: the social layer, who cheated, who hid in a bathroom, who left.

## E. The seeker side (under-served, gives variety)

**34. Playing seeker** · 6 slides
Hook: `You get the photos. Now find them.`
Arc: the feed, the reveal timer, thermal, sweep, pressure, and the fact that
you have to physically reach them.

**35. Reading the feed** · 5 slides
Hook: `Eight photos. One location.`
Arc: triangulating from backgrounds, light direction, and what is missing.

**36. Seeker items** · 6 slides
Hook: `Five ways to squeeze them.`
Arc: sweep, thermal, echo, pressure, lockdown, one per slide.

**37. The cooldown** · 4 slides
Hook: `Five minutes of nothing.`
Arc: what a good seeker does with the cooldown, which is plan rather than wait.

**38. Clearing the board** · 4 slides
Hook: `Nobody survives. That is the goal.`
Arc: seeker scoring, why blackouts count, the time bonus.

## F. World layer

**39. The map is your actual city** · 5 slides
Hook: `Those are real places.`
Arc: landmarks pulled from live map data, filtered to public ground only.

**40. Three kinds of diamond** · 5 slides
Hook: `Beacon. Waystation. Cache.`
Arc: one per type, what each gives, and the 30 metre and 5 second rule.

**41. A park at 2am does not exist** · 4 slides
Hook: `Closed places are closed.`
Arc: opening hours are respected, which is both a rule and a safety measure.

**42. Where landmarks never appear** · 5 slides
Hook: `The map deliberately has holes.`
Arc: no schools, no hospitals, no private property, no bars, no rail. This is
a trust-building post and it is genuinely differentiating.

## G. Progression and cosmetics

**43. Nothing you buy helps you win** · 5 slides
Hook: `You cannot buy an advantage.`
Arc: the ethical line. In a game played with real bodies, a purchasable edge
is an incentive to take a real risk. Strong positioning post.

**44. Season one: EXPOSURE** · 6 slides
Hook: `Fifty tiers. Zero advantages.`
Arc: the pass, the two tracks, what is actually in it.

**45. Buffs are earned by walking** · 5 slides
Hook: `Ghost ping. Static. Decoy. Grace.`
Arc: the hider pool, and that the only way to get them is to visit real places.

**46. One purchase kills ads forever** · 3 slides
Hook: `Buy anything. Ads stop. Permanently.`
Arc: unusual and worth stating plainly.

## H. Safety and trust (differentiator, not a disclaimer)

**47. What we do not collect** · 6 slides
Hook: `No face detection. Ever.`
Arc: no faces, no gallery access, no audio, photos deleted after 24 hours,
position not live to the seeker. This is a genuine competitive advantage and
should be marketed as one.

**48. The card you show a security guard** · 5 slides
Hook: `For when someone asks what you are doing.`
Arc: the offline explainer card, why it exists, why it works without signal.
Unexpected and memorable.

**49. Leaving is free** · 4 slides
Hook: `Quit any time. No penalty.`
Arc: SOS, no quitter flag, nobody is told you are weak. The product refuses to
socially punish leaving, and that is worth saying.

## I. Founder and positioning

**50. Why I built this** · 5 slides, founder video or talking head
Hook: `Every hide and seek app is the same.`
Arc: they all ship a map and a lobby code → hiding well was never the hard
part → what if you had to keep proving it → here is what that turned into →
join the beta.

## Production notes

- **Best five to make first:** 1, 2, 12, 21, 47. They cover the mechanic, the
  loss state, a listicle, the skill, and trust.
- **Post 42 and 47 early.** A game that sends teenagers to coordinates will get
  the safety question immediately. Answering it before it is asked is worth
  more than any feature post.
- **Slide 1 does all the work.** Everything after it is retention. If the hook
  needs more than six words, it is not a hook.
- **Rotate categories.** Three mechanic posts in a row reads like a manual.
  Alternate A, B, D, and drop an F or H post every fifth.
- Reuse hooks across TikTok as the opening line of a talking-head video. The
  arcs are already scripts.
