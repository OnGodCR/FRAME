# Hidewire: marketing handoff brief

Paste this whole file as the opening context for whichever agent is generating
marketing. It is written to be self-contained: an agent reading only this
should be able to write hooks, scripts, and carousel copy without seeing the
product.

---

## 1. What Hidewire is, in one line

Hide and seek for adults and teenagers, played across a real city, where hiding
well is not enough because every few minutes your phone makes you photograph
where you are hiding and sends it to the person hunting you.

## 2. The 30-second version

A private group of 3 to 20 friends starts a round with an invite code. One
person is the Seeker. Everyone else scatters across a zone, typically one
kilometre across, and has to survive 45 minutes.

The twist is the **check-in**. On a timer the whole group agrees to in advance,
every hider's phone goes off and they have 60 seconds to take two live photos,
back camera then front camera, of exactly where they are hiding. Those photos
go straight to the Seeker's feed. Miss the window, or fail the validity checks,
and you are **BLACKED OUT**: eliminated, publicly, in a way the game treats as
more embarrassing than simply being caught.

Meanwhile the Seeker gets everyone's GPS position, but only on a slow timer and
only for a few seconds at a time. The zone shrinks in the last third. Real
landmarks around you, pulled from live map data, can be visited for buffs.
Catching someone requires being physically close enough for a Bluetooth
handshake, so nobody gets tagged from across the street.

## 3. Why it is different, and what to lead with

**Lead with the photo mechanic. Never lead with the map.**

There is an existing category of GPS hide-and-seek apps (HideZone, Gottcha, the
Jet Lag companion apps). They all ship the same thing: a lobby code, a map,
periodic position reveals, a shrinking zone, a few items. That is a commodity.

Nobody else requires **photographic proof of concealment**. It changes the game
in three ways that are worth saying out loud in marketing:

1. **It punishes hiding in a hole.** In a GPS-only game the winning move is to
   find one good spot and never move. Here, every check-in is fresh evidence,
   and a hider who never moves leaks the same background over and over.
2. **It generates content.** Photo feeds are funny, screenshot-able, and
   shareable. GPS dots are not. This is the reason the game is marketable at
   all on a visual platform.
3. **There is a second skill.** Good players learn to compose a photo that is
   technically valid but tells the Seeker nothing. That is a genuinely fun
   thing to get better at, and a great subject for content.

## 4. The vocabulary (use these words, they are the brand)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Round** | One game. 20 to 120 minutes, usually 45. |
| **Check-in** | The forced photo. Back camera, then front camera, 60 seconds. |
| **BLACKED OUT** | Eliminated for missing or failing a check-in. Worse than being tagged. |
| **The Zone** | The circular play area. Shrinks late in the round. |
| **Reveal / getting pinged** | The Seeker receiving everyone's position on a timer. |
| **Tag** | A confirmed catch, requiring real physical proximity. |
| **Beacon / Waystation / Cache** | Real-world landmarks you can visit for buffs. |
| **FILM** | The soft currency. Cosmetics only. |
| **Season pass: EXPOSURE** | Season 01. 50 tiers, free and paid tracks. |

Tagline in use: **"Hiding isn't enough. Prove it."**

## 5. Voice and tone

Terse. Confident. Slightly cold. Closer to a piece of equipment than a party
game. Short declarative sentences. The game is genuinely a little stressful and
the copy should not apologise for that; the stress is the product.

**Do:** "Every five minutes, prove it." / "You have sixty seconds." / "The
frame is always one moment too late."

**Do not:** exclamation marks, "epic", "insane", "you won't BELIEVE", emoji
spam, or anything that sounds like a mobile game ad from 2016.

**Never use em-dashes.** Use periods, commas, colons, or the middle dot ( · )
that the app itself uses as a separator. This is a hard style rule.

## 6. Visual identity

- **Background:** near-black `#0A0A0C`. Everything sits on this.
- **Accent:** acid yellow-green `#C8FF2E`. Used sparingly and only for the
  thing that matters most on screen.
- **Text:** off-white `#F4F4F2`. Danger red `#FF4438`. Warning amber `#FFB020`.
- **Never** put white text on the acid green. The contrast is 1.18:1 and
  unreadable. On green, use the near-black.
- **Type:** Space Grotesk Bold for headlines. IBM Plex Mono for everything
  small, uppercase, with wide letter-spacing (around 1.5 to 2px at 10px). That
  letterspaced mono is the single most recognisable part of the look.
- **Logo:** four viewfinder corner brackets with a solid teardrop breaking out
  toward the top right. Acid green on near-black.
- **Motifs:** corner brackets framing things, rotated-45-degree diamonds, thin
  dashed circles, tabular countdown numbers, dark map with a bright zone ring.

## 7. Who this is for

13+, skewing 16 to 26. People who already play hide and seek in a mall as a
joke, who watch Jet Lag the Game, who organise things in a group chat. It is
played with **friends you already have**. There is no matchmaking and no
stranger discovery, deliberately. Marketing should read as "get your group to
do this," never "meet new people."

## 8. Content angles that will work

Ranked by how well they suit a carousel or a short video:

1. **The check-in is the hook.** A countdown, a phone going off, someone
   scrambling to photograph a bin they are crouched behind. This is the ad.
2. **"Best hiding spots that still pass the check"** and its inverse, "spots
   that will get you blacked out." Naturally listicle-shaped, naturally a
   carousel.
3. **How to take a photo that proves nothing.** The skill-expression angle.
   Genuinely useful, genuinely funny.
4. **Blackout compilations.** People losing badly. Highest shareability.
5. **The rules, explained in 6 cards.** Evergreen, saves well, good for the
   pinned post.
6. **Founder video: why I built this.** Works best as the counterweight to the
   AI-generated carousels; a real face buys credibility that generated
   graphics do not.
7. **City-specific:** "hiding in [city] is unfair because of this one park."
   Cheap to produce, strong local pull, good for seeding a launch city.

## 9. Things you must not claim

These are not preferences, they are legal and safety lines the product is
built around. Marketing that contradicts them creates real exposure.

- **Do not imply the app identifies people or recognises faces.** It does not,
  by design, and cannot. The photo checks look at brightness, blur, and detail
  only. Saying otherwise invites a biometric privacy claim.
- **Do not imply you can play with strangers.** Invite code only.
- **Do not suggest trespassing, hiding on private property, hiding near roads
  or train tracks, or playing in a vehicle.** The app actively suspends itself
  above 10 mph. Content showing unsafe hiding is off-limits regardless of how
  well it would perform.
- **Do not imply anything purchasable makes you win.** Nothing purchasable
  affects a round, and that is a deliberate ethical line worth stating, not
  hiding.
- **Do not promise photos are private.** They go to the Seeker. Say so plainly.
  What you can promise is that they are deleted 24 hours after the round and
  never enter match history.
- **Do not use "surveillance" framing.** The game is consensual and photos are
  of places, not people. Keep it that way in the language.

## 10. Current status, for honest copy

The app is a working demo, not a shipped product. There is no public download
yet. Marketing right now is building an audience ahead of a TestFlight beta,
so calls to action should be **"join the waitlist"** or **"comment to get in
the first beta,"** never "download now."

## Appendix: seed prompt for a carousel agent

> You are writing an Instagram and TikTok carousel for Hidewire, using the brief
> above. Produce N slides. Slide 1 is a hook of six words or fewer that would
> stop a thumb. Slides 2 to N-1 each carry one idea, at most 12 words of
> headline plus at most 25 words of body. The final slide is a call to action
> to join the beta waitlist.
>
> Every slide: near-black `#0A0A0C` background, off-white `#F4F4F2` text, acid
> green `#C8FF2E` on exactly one element per slide and no more. Headlines in a
> heavy geometric sans, all-caps labels in a monospace with wide letter
> spacing. 4:5 portrait, 1080x1350. Keep all text inside the middle 85 percent.
>
> No em-dashes. No exclamation marks. No emoji. Do not claim the app
> recognises faces or lets you play with strangers. Do not depict hiding on
> private property or near traffic.
